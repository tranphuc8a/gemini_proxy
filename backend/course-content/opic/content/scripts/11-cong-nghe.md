## A48 · Đồ vật người Việt Nam dùng nhiều nhất
- en: What is the object that people in your country use the most in their daily lives? Why?
- dang: y-kien

Without question, the object Vietnamese people use most in their daily lives is the mobile phone.
Vietnam has one of the highest smartphone penetration rates in Southeast Asia, and it's easy to see why.
People use their phones for virtually everything — calls, messaging, banking, shopping, navigation, entertainment, and food delivery.
Apps like Zalo for messaging, Grab for transport and delivery, and various banking apps have become essential daily tools.
Mobile payment is growing rapidly too — many people now pay for groceries, coffee, even street food using QR codes.
For younger generations especially, the smartphone is the central device for both work and social life.
It's genuinely hard to imagine daily life in Vietnam without one now.

## A50 · Kinh nghiệm đáng nhớ khi dùng điện thoại
- en: Tell me about a memorable experience you had with your phone.
- dang: kinh-nghiem

One really memorable experience happened about a year and a half ago.
I was jogging near my apartment when I slipped on a wet surface and dropped my phone into a shallow puddle.
The screen went black almost immediately.
I panicked.
I dried it off, tried turning it on — nothing.
I searched online and found the classic advice about putting it in uncooked rice to absorb the moisture.
I left it overnight, and by the next morning it turned back on.
I was genuinely relieved — and a little amazed, honestly.
The screen glitched for a few days but eventually returned to normal.
After that I immediately bought a waterproof case and a screen protector.
It taught me how fragile and valuable these devices really are.

## A51 · Vấn đề với điện thoại (hỏng/sự cố), cách bạn giải quyết

- dang: kinh-nghiem

The battery was draining and there was no way to charge it.
Since it was still under warranty, I took it to the official gemini service center.
After running diagnostics, they found the charging port was faulty due to a manufacturing defect.

Now I make a habit of backing up data to cloud storage regularly — just in case.

## A53 · So sánh Internet hiện tại và quá khứ
- en: Compare how people use the internet now with how they used it in the past.
- dang: so-sanh

The contrast between internet use now and 10 to 15 years ago is pretty extraordinary.
In the past, access in Vietnam was slow, expensive, and mostly limited to PC cafés or schools.
People used it mainly for basic browsing, email, or chatting on Yahoo Messenger.
Accessing news took time, and streaming anything was practically impossible.
Today, high-speed internet is widely available, affordable, and accessible on any smartphone, pretty much anywhere in the country.
Video calling family in perfect quality, streaming any film instantly, working remotely without technical limitations — all normal now.
The downsides are information overload, privacy concerns, and addictive usage patterns.
But on balance, the improvements to daily life and economic opportunity have been enormously positive.

## A54 · Giới thiệu về SNS (mạng xã hội), SNS hay dùng và những mặt tiêu cực
- en: Tell me about social networking services. Which ones do you use? What are the negative sides of SNS?
- dang: y-kien

Social networking services have become a really integral part of modern life in Vietnam.
The most popular platforms here are Facebook, TikTok, Instagram, and Zalo — which is a local messaging and social app.
I personally use Facebook to stay connected with friends and family, and scroll through Instagram occasionally for photography and tech content.
SNS has genuine benefits — maintaining relationships across distances, staying informed, even growing small businesses.
But the negatives are significant and shouldn't be ignored.
The most serious problem is misinformation.
False news spreads faster than corrections, and many people believe things without verifying them.
Cyberbullying is another major concern, especially for young people.
Excessive social media use has also been linked to anxiety and lower self-esteem.
Personally, I try to limit my daily usage and be selective about what I engage with.
