## A60 · Giới thiệu về ngân hàng gần nhà / ấn tượng về ngân hàng nào nhất
- en: Tell me about a bank near your home. Which bank impressed you the most, and why?
- dang: mieu-ta

The bank I use most is Vietcombank — there's a branch about 10 minutes from my apartment.
It's a fairly modern branch with a comfortable waiting area and a well-organized queuing system.
I've visited a few times for things like account updates or processing a larger transfer that needed in- person verification.
The staff have been professional and helpful every time.
But honestly, what impresses me most is their mobile banking app.
It's fast, secure, and covers almost every transaction I need — transfers, bill payments, card management.
Since I started using it, I rarely need to visit the branch at all.
The combination of reliable in-person service and a great digital platform is what makes Vietcombank stand out for me.

## A61 · Bị vấn đề gì nên vào bệnh viện? Ký ức về việc đó / Ai giới thiệu?
- en: Tell me about a time you went to the hospital. What was the problem? Who recommended the hospital or the doctor?
- dang: kinh-nghiem

About two years ago, I had to visit the hospital due to persistent stomach pain that lasted for several days.
At first I figured it was something minor — bad food, maybe — so I rested and drank plenty of water.
But when the pain got worse and started affecting my sleep, a colleague really encouraged me to get checked properly.
She recommended Dr. Hung at Viet Duc Hospital, saying he'd helped her with something similar before.
I went the next morning.
After an examination and some tests, Dr. Hung diagnosed a mild gastric inflammation, likely from stress and irregular eating habits.
He prescribed medication and dietary adjustments for three weeks.
What stood out was his patience — he explained everything clearly and answered all my questions without making me feel rushed.
The treatment worked well, and that experience taught me to take my eating habits more seriously and not delay seeking medical help.

## A63 · Lần đầu đi cắt tóc: khi nào, với ai, cảm giác như thế nào?
- en: Tell me about the first time you went to get a haircut. When was it, who were you with, and how did you feel?
- dang: kinh-nghiem

My first independent haircut was when I was about 17, the summer before starting university.
Until then, my mother had always come with me to the barber and handled the communication.
This time I decided to go alone and manage it myself.
I walked into a small barbershop near our house and tried to explain what I wanted — shorter on the sides, some length on top.
Unfortunately, I was too vague and a bit nervous, so the barber made his own interpretation.
When I looked in the mirror... the result was much shorter than I'd imagined.
My eyes went wide.
I didn't say anything because I felt too awkward, paid, and left.
My friends and family teased me about it for weeks.
Looking back, it was a small but important lesson: communicate clearly and specifically — and don't be afraid to speak up.

## A64 · Kỷ niệm không tốt liên quan đến cắt tóc và cách xử lý

A more recent bad haircut experience happened about two years ago at a new barbershop I was trying for the first time.

He nodded confidently and said he could do it.
The end result looked nothing like the photo.

I paid, left, and resolved not to go back.
The lesson: even with a clear photo, only go to a barber who comes recommended or whose work you've seen before.

## A65 · Các loại giấy tờ tùy thân ở quốc gia bạn
- en: What kinds of identification documents do people have in your country? How do you get one?
- dang: mieu-ta

In Vietnam, the primary ID is the CCCD — the Citizen Identification Card — which replaced the old system a few years ago.
Every citizen aged 14 and above is required to have one.
It contains your name, date of birth, address, nationality, and a unique 12-digit ID number.
It's also embedded with a chip storing biometric data, making it more secure than older ID systems.
You need it in almost every official situation — opening a bank account, signing contracts, booking flights, registering a SIM card, applying for jobs.
To get one, you go to your local police station with supporting documents.
They take a photo and fingerprints, and the card is usually issued within 7 to 14 days.
If it's damaged or has incorrect information, you return to the police station and apply for a replacement.
The process is straightforward, so it's important to store the card safely and report errors immediately.
