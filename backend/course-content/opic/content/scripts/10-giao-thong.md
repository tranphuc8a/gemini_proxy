## A36 · Ở nước bạn chủ yếu dùng phương tiện gì?
- en: What kinds of transportation do people use the most in your country?
- dang: mieu-ta

In Vietnam, the most widely used form of transport is definitely the motorbike.
Almost every household owns at least one — it's affordable, cheap to run, and perfect for navigating narrow streets and heavy traffic.
In Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City, you can see thousands of motorbikes on the road at any given time.
Public buses exist and are fairly cheap, but the routes aren't always convenient, so not everyone relies on them.
Ride-hailing apps like Grab have become huge in recent years — offering both motorbike and car options.
I personally commute by motorbike most days since my office is only about 15 minutes away.
And Hanoi's metro recently opened a new line, which is a really positive development for reducing congestion.

## A37 · So sánh phương tiện giao thông ngày xưa và bây giờ
- en: How has transportation in your country changed compared to the past?
- dang: so-sanh

Transportation in Vietnam has changed dramatically over the past two or three decades.
When my parents were young, bicycles were the primary way of getting around — to school, to the market, to work.
Slow, but practical and eco-friendly.
Then as the economy grew rapidly in the 90s and 2000s, motorbikes became affordable for most households and quickly replaced bicycles.
Today, the streets are dominated by motorbikes, with a growing number of private cars as well.
Ride-hailing apps and GPS navigation have also made commuting much more convenient.
The downside is severe traffic congestion and air pollution in major cities.
I think the future needs to prioritize public transport, cycling infrastructure, and electric vehicles to make cities more sustainable.

## A38 · Vấn đề gặp phải với phương tiện giao thông và cách giải quyết
- en: Tell me about a problem you have had with transportation. How did you deal with it?
- dang: kinh-nghiem

The most common problem I face is traffic congestion during rush hours.
My commute normally takes 15 minutes, but during peak hours — around 7:30 to 8:30 AM — it can stretch to 40 minutes or more.
That's both time-consuming and stressful, especially with an early morning meeting.
My solution is to leave home earlier, around 7 AM, to avoid the worst of it.
Another issue is unexpected rain while riding without a raincoat.
I now always keep a compact foldable raincoat in my bag during the rainy season.
I once had a flat tyre on the way to work too, which was stressful because I wasn't near a repair shop.
Luckily a nearby resident pointed me to one just two streets away.
That experience taught me to have repair shop locations saved on my phone — just in case.

## A39 · Ký ức không tốt liên quan tới phương tiện giao thông
- en: Tell me about an unpleasant memory related to transportation. What happened?
- dang: kinh-nghiem

One unpleasant memory happened about a year ago in the summer.
I was riding home from work when I got caught in a sudden, very heavy rainstorm.
I hadn't checked the forecast, and I had no raincoat with me.
The rain was so intense the road flooded in several places and visibility was really poor.
I had to pull over under a small overhang and wait for about 45 minutes, completely soaked.
What made it worse was that my phone battery died, so I couldn't call anyone or check a map.
By the time I got home, my clothes and shoes were completely waterlogged, and I ended up catching a cold the next day.
Genuinely miserable experience.
But I learned from it — now I always check the weather before heading out and carry a raincoat during the wet season.

## B083 · Người nước bạn đi lại bằng gì? Phương tiện phổ biến là gì?
- en: Tell me about how people get around in your country, Do they drive their own cars or take trains. What are some typical means of transportation to get around?
- dang: mieu-ta

There are various types of transportation in my country.
Some people take public buses or trains to get around the city, while others use their private cars.
When traveling to a faraway place, people can take a train or an airplane, but those are not very popular in Vietnam.
The most popular vehicle is the motorbike.
Most Vietnamese people use it as their private vehicle to go to work or travel around.
Currently, it is the most suitable option considering the road conditions, traffic, and lifestyle in Vietnam.

## B084 · Bạn đi lại bằng phương tiện gì? Tự lái hay đi phương tiện công cộng?
- en: What means of transportation do you use to get around? Do you drive or take public transportation?
- dang: thoi-quen

I have one car and one motorbike, but I usually ride my bike to work or get around.
My workplace is not far from my home, so I use my bike to save gasoline and also to protect the environment.
When I go to my hometown or need to travel somewhere far, I drive my car instead, because it is much safer for long-distance trips.
So most of the time, I ride my bike to get around.

## B085 · Hồi nhỏ bạn đi lại thế nào? Giao thông khi đó khác gì bây giờ?
- en: How did you travel when you were a child? Were the type of transportation different back then? Describe for me how people used to get around in your city or town?
- dang: so-sanh

When I was a kid, transportation was not as good as it is now.
There were only a few cars and buses, and they were usually very old and ran very slowly.
It was inconvenient for people when they wanted to travel far.
But now, there are so many vehicles.
I can see a lot of cars, buses, and motorbikes moving on the road.
The road and traffic infrastructure have also become much better than in the past.
It is very convenient and easy to get from place to place.
Even though there are sometimes traffic jams, they don't have too much impact on people nowadays.
